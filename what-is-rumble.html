What is Rumble?

The full story of Rumble, Inc. — history, products, policies, and milestones. Compiled from public sources including Wikipedia.

Rumble, Inc. is a Canadian-American online video platform, web hosting, and cloud services business headquartered in Toronto, Canada, with U.S. headquarters in Longboat Key, Florida. Founded in 2013 by Chris Pavlovski, Rumble’s cloud services business hosts Truth Social. The video platform is popular among American conservative and far-right users, and Rumble has been described as “alt-tech.”

History

  • October 30, 2013: Rumble founded as an alternative to YouTube for independent vloggers and smaller creators. Early years saw limited popularity.
  • From 2020: a large influx of viewership at the start of the COVID-19 pandemic — monthly visitors rose from 1.6 million in 2020 to 31.9 million by 2021.
  • January 11, 2021: Rumble filed an antitrust lawsuit against Google over search algorithms, seeking damages exceeding $2 billion. A California judge ruled in August 2022 that the case could proceed.
  • May 2021: investment from Peter Thiel, Vivek Ramaswamy, and JD Vance at a valuation around $500 million.
  • October 2021: acquired Locals.
  • December 2021: Trump Media & Technology Group announced a wide-ranging technology and cloud services agreement with Rumble.
  • August 2022: announced Rumble Ads, with Truth Social as its first publisher.
  • September 2022: became publicly traded on Nasdaq under ticker RUM after its SPAC merger.
  • May 2023: acquired the podcasting platform CallIn.
  • 2023: granted exclusive rights to the online stream of the Republican presidential primary debates.
  • November 2024: Guy “Dr Disrespect” Beahm signed a deal to produce free and exclusive content and head Rumble’s gaming division, including equity.
  • January 2025: Chris Pavlovski became a billionaire after Rumble’s stock increased nearly 190% in 2024.
  • November 2025: signed a business combination agreement to acquire Northern Data AG in an all-stock deal, adding approximately 22,000 Nvidia GPUs and globally distributed data centers; expected to close Q2 2026.

Locals

  • Creator crowdfunding site co-founded by Dave Rubin and Assaf Lev, based in Miami; founded after Rubin and Jordan Peterson left Patreon.
  • Raised just over $1 million from 10 506(b) private placement investors in March 2020, plus $3.8 million on April 20, 2021, led by Craft Ventures.
  • Uses a paywall approach. Assaf Lev is president and CEO; Andrew Conru is director. Acquired by Rumble in October 2021. Website: locals.com.

Design and restrictions

  • Rumble features “recommended channels” and an “Earnings” tab, and lets users generate revenue from videos — uploads can be licensed to partners such as Yahoo! and Microsoft News, with earnings deposited into the user’s Rumble account.
  • Terms forbid pornography, harassment, racism, antisemitism, and copyright infringement, as well as illegal content.
  • Rumble built its own cloud service infrastructure and video streaming capacity.

Users and content

  • Popular among conservatives and far-right users; described as part of “alt-tech.” Researchers noted creators who gained an audience after being removed from YouTube or Facebook.
  • August 2022: 78 million monthly active users; Andrew Tate began posting on Rumble, driving a significant increase in downloads.
  • May 2022 Pew Research: 20% of American adults had heard of Rumble; 2% regularly got news from it. Of regular users, 76% were Republican or Republican-leaning.
  • Rumble did not ban Russian state media after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine; it was blocked in France in November 2022 after refusing to remove Russian state media accounts, and blocked in Russia in May 2024 for not complying with a demand to remove content.
  • Early 2023: began hosting live broadcasts for Thrill One Sports & Entertainment leagues (Nitrocross, Street League Skateboarding, Power Slap). Removed access in Brazil in 2023 over legal orders; back online in Brazil in early 2025.
  • February 2025: joined a lawsuit against Brazil’s Supreme Federal Court justice Alexandre de Moraes over alleged illegal censorship of right-wing media; Moraes ordered Rumble’s suspension on February 21, 2025.

Platform features

  • Video hosting and discovery: direct uploads in high-definition formats, scheduled premieres, title/description/tags/thumbnail editing. No built-in trimming/cropping — editing requires re-upload.
  • Live streaming via Direct RTMP, multi-platform streaming to YouTube and Twitch, multi-guest sessions, live chat, and clip sharing.
  • Creator tools: analytics dashboard with hourly updates, subscription monetization, API access, custom embeds, oEmbed, and Rumble Studio for live events.
  • Licensing options: Exclusive Video Management, Video Management (Excluding YouTube), Rumble Player License, and Personal Use.
  • Revenue sharing: 60% of net ad revenue to creators; $50 payout threshold via PayPal, bank transfer, or wire. Rumble Premium: $9.99/month or $99/year.
  • Ad formats: pre-roll, mid-roll, host-read, and lower thirds. Partnerships include Cumulus Media and Westwood One; enterprise licensing with MTV and Yahoo.
  • Content moderation is minimalist and free-speech oriented, prohibiting illegal activities, child exploitation, and direct incitements to violence.
  • Infrastructure: Rumble Cloud, proprietary CDN, independent processing and global distribution. Rumble states it does not sell personal information, with no targeted advertising or user profiling.
  • User base growth: 1.6M MAU (Q3 2020) → 36M MAU (Q3 2021) → 59M average global MAUs (Q1 2025).
